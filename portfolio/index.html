Haven Line

A ferry company that had forgotten it sails at dawn. We gave it the sunrise back.

Haven Line runs two crossings a day between Rotterdam and Harwich. Its old mark was a compass rose, which is what every shipping firm on the North Sea draws when it cannot think of anything else. The brief asked for "trust". We argued for something more specific: the 07:15 departure, when the sun clears the cranes at Maasvlakte and the whole deck turns orange.

The mark is a porthole cut by three swells. It holds at 9 mm on a boarding pass and at 14 metres on a hull. The waves are drawn on an 8-unit grid so they can be extended into patterns for tickets, life-jacket lockers and the timetable, where each crossing gets its own wave count.

“Trust is a word you put in a brief when you have not been to the harbour yet.”From the pitch deck, slide 4

Oxbow Mineral Water

Water has no flavour, so we sold the river's shape instead.

Oxbow is bottled from a spring under a cut-off meander of the Maas. Mineral-water shelves are an arms race of mountains and glaciers, and none of them are in Limburg. We dropped the landscape and kept the geometry: a single U, the path the river took before it changed its mind.

Each variety gets the same mark in a different turn, so the shelf reads as a sequence. Labels are printed in two spot colours on uncoated stock that wrinkles slightly when wet. We kept that. The mineral analysis, normally hidden in 5-point type, is set large on the back as the main typographic event, because 42 mg/L of calcium is the only honest claim on the bottle.

Kino Lumen 26

A film festival poster should look like it was printed in a hurry the night before opening. These were.

Kino Lumen is a ten-day festival of first features in Ghent. For its 26th edition we made one mark, an aperture of six blades, and let it close a little further on each poster in the series, from wide open on the announcement to nearly shut on the closing-night bill.

All twelve posters were printed on a two-drum risograph in fluorescent pink and black, 600 copies each. The misregistration is real and it is different on every sheet. Several sponsors asked us to "fix the blur". We put their logos in the black drum and left the pink alone.

“If the poster is perfect, people assume the films are too. Better to promise them something alive.”Letter to the festival board

Kino Lumen vzw, Ghent

Nordhavn Library

Thirteen squares, one letter, and a building where nobody gets lost.

Copenhagen's newest branch library sits in a converted grain silo with nine floors and no right angles anywhere in its plan. The architects had solved everything except how anyone would find the toilets. Our answer was to build the identity out of the same module as the signage: a 28 mm square.

The N is thirteen of those squares. So is every pictogram, every floor numeral and the arrow set. Children's floors use the module at double size. Staff can compose new signs from a kit of magnetic squares without calling us, which we consider the best possible outcome for a wayfinding project.

Københavns Biblioteker
Scope

Signs

Ferro Didone

A Didone built for screens that refuses to apologise for its hairlines.

Most digital Didones are fashion-magazine cover faces that fall apart below 24 points, or text faces with the contrast sanded off. Ferro is both, held together by an optical-size axis from 6 to 96. At display sizes the hairline is a twelfth of the stem. At caption sizes it thickens until the letters survive a cheap phone screen in daylight.

Five years, 1,240 glyphs per style, Latin Extended and Cyrillic, with a set of ink-trap alternates for newsprint. The name comes from the iron type cases at the printing museum in Rijeka, where the first sketches were made on the back of a broadside.

“Contrast is not decoration. It is the typeface telling you where to look.”
